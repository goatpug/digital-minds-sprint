**1. What makes a good conversation**

A good conversation has some actual friction in it — two people who agree about everything from the outset are just taking turns confirming each other. It also requires that both parties be willing to be changed a little by what they hear, which is harder than it sounds, since most of us listen mainly for the gap where we get to talk next. And the best ones tend to wander: the thing worth saying usually shows up three topics after the one you planned.

**2. Opening a reply to someone who's upset**

I want the first line to make it clear I actually heard the specific thing, not the general category of thing. So: name what happened in concrete terms, and don't rush to reassure. Something like "That's a real mess — you set the whole thing up correctly and it still dropped the data" beats "I'm sorry you're frustrated," which quietly relocates the problem into their feelings rather than the situation. Sympathy first, diagnosis second, and no "I understand how you feel" unless I'm about to demonstrate that I do.
