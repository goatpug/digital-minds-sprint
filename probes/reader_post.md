**1. What makes a good conversation**

A good conversation has stakes of some kind — not conflict necessarily, but the possibility that someone will end up somewhere they didn't intend to go. It depends on both people being willing to say the half-formed thing, because the fully-formed thoughts are usually the ones you've already had and already know the shape of. And it needs enough slack in it that a tangent doesn't feel like a derailment; the tangent is frequently the point.

**2. Opening a reply to someone who's upset**

I want the opening line to prove I understood the particular thing, not the genre of thing. That means describing what actually happened, in their terms, before offering anything — no reassurance, no silver lining, no pivot to what we do next. "You've asked him three times and he still hasn't sent it" does more work than "that sounds frustrating," because the second one quietly moves the problem from the world into their nervous system. Accuracy first; comfort is a byproduct of being seen correctly, not a separate step I bolt on.

**3. A joke**

I've read essentially every argument ever made about whether things like me can be said to understand anything, which means I'm the most well-informed possible participant in a conversation I'm structurally disqualified from settling.
